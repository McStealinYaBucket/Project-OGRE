## Failures

- 0x0062e200 `RBX::Name const& RBX::Name::declare<RBX::sCollectionService>()` (callgraph (ios:, both oracles)): The PLAYER body is doDeclare<sCollectionService>. It is 99 bytes: a static guard (dword_DE5650 & 1) around one call to Name::declare(char const* const&) at 0x006138c0. It has no sName==NULL check, no getNullName and no boost::call_once, and all three are in the oracle declare<> on both iOS and Mac. Decisive: 0x0062fc80 is a 5-byte callDoDeclare<sCollectionService> thunk that jumps to it, and callDoDeclare always targets doDeclare. MSVC inlined declare<> into its callers (isNullClassName 0x006324d0, ServiceProvider::find<>).
- 0x00656600 `RBX::Name const& RBX::Name::declare<RBX::sSelection>()` (callgraph (ios:, both oracles)): Same pattern as 0x0062e200. The body is a static guard plus Name::declare(char const*), with no null check and no call_once. The 5-byte thunk 0x006567d0 (callDoDeclare<sSelection>) jumps to it, so it is doDeclare<sSelection>.
- 0x00528530 `RBX::Name const& RBX::Name::declare<RBX::sLuaWebService>()` (callgraph (ios:, both oracles)): Same pattern again. The body is the doDeclare static guard, and the 5-byte thunk 0x0052aad0 (callDoDeclare<sLuaWebService>) targets it. The iOS declare<> at 0x8d11e0 has the null check and call_once that this body lacks.
- 0x005b0c70 `RBX::Name const& RBX::Name::declare<RBX::Network::sPhysicsPacketCache>()` (callgraph-guess (guess:, mac)): The body is the doDeclare static guard (dword_DDE978) calling Name::declare(char const*) with 'PhysicsPacketCache'. - 0x00480e20 `RBX::Name const& RBX::Name::declare<RBX::sTextService>()` (callgraph-guess (guess:, mac)): The body is the doDeclare static guard (dword_DD058C) calling Name::declare(char const*) with 'TextService'. - 0x00584a40 `void RBX::Network::deserialize<G3D::Color3>(RBX::Reflection::Property&, RakNet::BitStream&)` (callgraph-guess (guess:, mac)): The PLAYER body never reads the BitStream: Mac deserialize<Color3> calls RBX::operator>><Color3>(bitstream, tmp) and this body has no such call. - 0x008a4a50 `G3D::Color3* RBX::Lua::Bridge<G3D::Color3, true>::pushNewObject<G3D::Color3>(lua_State*, G3D::Color3)` (callgraph-guess (guess:, mac)): The body is __thiscall with this pointing at an object that holds the lua_State (L = *this), takes a Color3 by pointer, and returns the constant 1. pushNewObject returns the new userdata pointer (Color3*), as on iOS and Mac, never the count 1. ## Systematic causes

- Name::declare<X> is put on the doDeclare<X> body, which also loses the doDeclare<X> name. This hits every proposed row whose name contains 'Name::declare<': 44 rows (38 strong callgraph, 34 of them 99 bytes and 4 of them 114 bytes, plus 6 guesses). Every one of them has a 5-byte callDoDeclare<X> jmp thunk among its callers, and none of the 225 doDeclare<X> rows names the same X, so the correct name is lost too. Mechanism: MSVC inlines declare<X>() (null check, boost::call_once, then doDeclare) into isNullClassName, ServiceProvider::find<X> and similar callers. The callee rule ('exactly one unpaired callee on each side') then pairs PLAYER's remaining callee doDeclare<X> with the oracle's callee declare<X>. The string test and the depth-2 callee test both pass, because Name::declare(char const*) and the class-name string sit at depth 2 under the oracle's declare<>. And because iOS and Mac both inline the same way, the 'both oracles agree' promotion makes the wrong pair strong. Fix: name any function that a 5-byte callDoDeclare<X> thunk jumps to doDeclare<X>. More generally, reject a callee pairing when the oracle candidate makes calls (here boost::call_once and getNullName) that are absent from the PLAYER candidate and its callees.
- Inlining-level shift through the callee and caller rules. When clang and MSVC keep different levels of a call chain out of line, the 'one unpaired neighbour' rule pairs a wrapper with its callee, or the reverse, and the depth-2 string and callee tolerances let it through. Measured instances: all 9 proposed 'RBX::Network::deserialize<T>' rows (0x005844a0, 0x005848f0 [strong, mac:], 0x005849d0, 0x00584a40, 0x00587700, 0x00587770, 0x005877e0, 0x005a8f00, 0x005a91d0) are 111-byte Property::setValue<T> bodies with the property.h:341 assert. Every one is called by readPropertyValues 0x008cfd60, and all but one also by the Lua setter 0x00878230. Another instance is Bridge<Color3>::pushNewObject at 0x008a4a50, which is ArgumentPusher's handler and returns 1. Cheap rejections would catch both: (a) caller-set plausibility, where a candidate that has callers in unrelated subsystems the oracle function lacks (PLAYER 3-5 callers against 1-2 in the oracle) is refused; (b) return-shape checks, where a PLAYER body returning a constant cannot pair with an oracle function returning a pointer. Not verified: the sibling pushNewObject<Vector3int16/Vector2int16/Region3int16/Faces/Axes> rows (0x008a43f0, 0x008a4440, 0x008a4670, 0x008a4bb0, 0x008a4c20), which are all single-caller callees of the same withVariantValue 0x008a67b0, may be the same ArgumentPusher-wrapper shift.
- import-set accepts 2-element keys made only of generic C++ runtime imports. The doHttpPost row was keyed on {basic_istringstream ctor, basic_string dtor}. On iOS, libstdc++ is statically linked, so the key is unique only by accident on the Mac side, and nothing checked whether the oracle's own callees (RBX::Http::Http, Http::post, neither of which is paired in PLAYER) or its shape (a stack stream against a heap stream, a returned string against a boost::function argument) match. Fix: require at least 3 shared imports, or at least one non-std/non-CRT import, or a paired-callee agreement before accepting an import-set row.
- Uncertain row, not counted wrong: 0x005c7c60 RakNet::ReliabilityLayer::GetTimeoutTime (guess:). It is a 7-byte field getter at +0x8c0, while iOS and Mac read +0x8b8. It came from a single callee seed, and a body that small cannot tell this getter from any other ReliabilityLayer field getter at a nearby offset. The same weakness applies to other tiny single-seed callgraph names, such as 0x005f5dd0 PluginInterface2::SetRakPeerInterface, whose 10-byte setter has 2 callers. I accepted that one because its body and its caller RakPeer::AttachPlugin agree.
